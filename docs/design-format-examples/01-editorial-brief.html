Phase 1 partial

Make full-rules self-play stable.

A8 has proved that the sparse-reward PPO system can learn on full generated Heat tracks. The next decision is no longer whether learning exists—it is how to stabilise it without tuning away the evidence.

Direction A · Sprint A8 · updated 13 July 2026

Executive decision

Use the 1M run as the baseline. Stabilise one variable at a time.

The 50k pilot was underpowered. At roughly one million nominal transitions, three seeds produced useful held-out play—especially at four and six seats—but no single checkpoint or seed is reliable across the full grid.

Recommended next move. Split evaluation into a development validation band and a fresh untouched test band. Compare one stabilisation change against the unchanged 1M baseline before adding new targets or architecture.

What changed our mind

  • Three seeds completed in under 45 minutes of CPU training.
  • Aggregate play clears chance against weak fields at four and six seats.
  • The sparse signal is sufficient to learn; scale, not reward absence, explained the pilot.
  • Checkpoint quality oscillates, making selection and reproducibility the binding problem.

Risks carried forward

  • Validation overfitting: milestone inspection has already consumed one held-out band.
  • Bad yardstick: the nominally strong heuristic is poorly calibrated on generated tracks.
  • Seed sensitivity: two-seat performance remains materially less stable than larger fields.

A clean exit from A8

A8 is complete when a pre-declared checkpoint rule produces stable, positive held-out placement reward across seeds and seat counts, and the final claim survives a fresh test band that was not used for iteration.
