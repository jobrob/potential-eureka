01 · Objective and baseline

Move the stable A5 recipe to full generated Heat.

  • flat observation encoder;

The scale run added exact completed-game totals and saved and evaluated checkpoints at declared transition milestones. PyTorch is seeded before policy construction, making a seed control the entire training trajectory. Byte-identical seed-0 300k checkpoints from independent runs confirm reproducibility.

A7 evaluator correction

A7 previously seeded game and deck randomness but did not independently seed the policy's sampled actions. Exact policy results could therefore depend on ambient PyTorch RNG state and evaluation order.

The evaluator now gives every game a deterministic policy-sampling seed, preserves the caller's RNG state, and produces identical repeated reports. A regression test covers reproducibility and RNG neutrality. Policy tables produced before this correction are superseded wherever exact values matter; heuristic-only A7 results are unaffected.

04a · S1 gentler PPO result

Five epochs improve skill, but barely change oscillation.

S1 ran three matched one-million-transition seeds with n_epochs=5. All other training settings were unchanged. Each run saved 300k, 500k, 750k, and 1M checkpoints and evaluated them deterministically on the frozen 20-track validation grid with 50 games per opponent × seat cell. Training took 36.7 minutes and the full train-and-evaluate campaign took 42.0 minutes, below the 90-minute cap. Every seed completed more than 5,200 games. Following the gate, both A8Config and the A8 training CLI now default to five epochs while preserving an explicit override.

05 · Interpretation

The remaining problem is stability, not proof of learning.

The scale hypothesis is confirmed: 50k was much too early, and thousands rather than hundreds of games were needed before the current PPO system showed useful held-out play. This is still far below the millions of self-play games associated with AlphaZero-style systems; PPO is extracting a signal at this scale.

Learning is oscillatory. Individual 300k, 500k, 750k, and 1M checkpoints sometimes improve and then regress before recovering. The next issue is therefore training stability and checkpoint selection, not proof that the environment or reward is incapable of learning.

Why A8 is not complete

  • Seed sensitivity.
    Two-player performance varies materially by seed.
  • No dominant milestone.
    No single checkpoint wins at every seat count.
  • Unreliable yardstick.
    The “strong” generated-track heuristic is poorly calibrated.
  • Consumed holdout.
    The same held-out band was used to inspect milestones and must not become a repeatedly tuned final test set.
06 · Recommended next step

Separate evaluation roles, then change one variable.

Keep this scaled run as the baseline. Before selecting “best” checkpoints or tuning against them, split evaluation into a reusable validation band for development and a fresh untouched test band for final claims. The current 900000+ band has already been inspected and should become validation data, not the source of the final A8 claim.

What “freeze the rule” means

Choose checkpoints using one declared aggregate over the validation grid, rather than selecting a different milestone for whichever table is being discussed. The recommended primary score is mean placement reward averaged equally across 2-, 4-, and 6-seat cells and across the frozen opponent ladder. Placement reward uses every finishing position and is therefore less volatile than first-place rate, especially with six players. Report win rate and Wilson intervals as supporting evidence, and use them as a tie-break only if two checkpoints have effectively equal placement scores.

07 · Improving the strong heuristic

Neither repaired heuristic nor static search earns a universal “strong” label.

H1 repaired the severe recovery defect but did not create a calibrated rung. H2 then selected a safe, fast one-round search candidate; B1 implemented it and ran 200 games per opponent × seat cell. StaticSearchV1 confidently beats the repaired heuristic at 2, 4, and 6 seats, yet posts negative placement reward against the weak field at all three seat counts. This is strong evidence of matchup non-transitivity, not a total difficulty ladder.

B1 decision. Do not promote StaticSearchV1 or rename it as the strong benchmark. Keep its exact configuration available only through the explicit --eval-search-candidate option. Preserve the public StrongHeuristicAgent class name for compatibility, but label it heuristic_repaired in new A8 reports. The next benchmark design should aggregate several fixed rulers while retaining each per-style result. Read the full implemented result.
B2 implemented that recommendation. Six selected checkpoints from G0002 (S1 five epochs) and G0003 (S2 fixed anchor) ran against weak, repaired, search, and historical-policy rulers at 2, 4, and 6 seats: 72 cells and 14,400 races. G0003 raised the recipe-median aggregate reward from +0.097 to +0.159, and all four ruler medians improved. The +0.062 lift passes the predeclared breadth gate, although one of three matched runs regressed. Adopt the suite; prefer G0003 for selected-checkpoint skill while retaining the earlier rejection of anchoring as a stabilization fix. Read the full B2 result.
Important limitation. A single heuristic must not become the definition of success. Optimizing repeatedly against one scripted style can reward exploitation of its blind spots without improving general play. The heuristic should be one rung in an opponent suite alongside the weak agent, frozen historical policy checkpoints, and cross-seed policy matches.

Suggested strong-heuristic work

  1. Diagnose
    losses by track and decision type
  2. Repair
    the largest generated-track failure
  3. Gate
    against weak and current strong
  4. Freeze
    version and evaluation tracks

Do not begin by retuning many evaluator constants. First replay a fixed batch of losses and attribute the decisive mistakes: unsafe corner entry, excess heat conservation near the finish, poor gear/card joint choice, missed slipstream or blocking value, or failure on particular length/corner/lane combinations. The agent already contains joint planning, forward heat solvency, a relative objective, and rank-dependent risk posture; the useful question is which of those models fails on generated tracks, not which feature can be added next.

  • Generalization gate.
    On a heuristic-development band that is disjoint from ML validation and final test, the candidate must beat HeuristicAgent above chance at 2, 4, and 6 seats using a predeclared confidence bound.
  • Regression gate.
    The candidate must also beat or clearly outrank the current strong agent, while not increasing spin-outs or breaking legality, determinism, seat neutrality, and latency limits.
  • Freeze the opponent.
    Once the gates pass, name the version and stop tuning it during the stabilization comparison. Otherwise the ruler changes at the same time as the policy.
  • Keep several rulers.
    Evaluate against weak, strong-v2, an early fixed policy anchor, and frozen policies from other seeds. Agreement across those opponents is much stronger evidence than exploiting one script.

How this improves the A8 decision

A difficulty ladder makes plateau and regression easier to distinguish. A checkpoint that beats weak play but not strong-v2 has learned basic competence; one that improves against strong-v2 and historical policies has broader evidence of progress. The fixed early-policy anchor separately answers whether training is climbing beyond its earlier self. None of these replaces the untouched track test: opponent generalization and track generalization are different claims.

08 · Scaling experiment throughput

Use CPU parallelism now; batch rollouts before expecting a GPU win.

The code already accepts --device cuda, and PPO minibatch updates can run on the GPU. However, the hot rollout path currently calls the policy with a batch of one state, immediately copies the sampled result back to Python, advances a sequential rules engine, and repeats. The network is small. On that shape of workload, kernel-launch and CPU↔GPU synchronization overhead can outweigh the neural-network work. The scripted heuristics and game rules are Python control flow and cannot be moved to the GPU by changing the device flag.

Proposed batched collector

The detailed T2 design narrows the first implementation. Begin inside one game by batching the already-simultaneous GEAR and CARDS choices across active policy seats, then replay the sampled actions through the unchanged driver. This preserves whole-game collection and the current per-seat GAE contract while reducing batch-of-one policy calls.

Do not begin with 16–32 complete games. P0 recorded only 4.24 games per 1,024-step iteration, and a six-seat game averaged 613.7 transitions. A large pool would multiply the PPO rollout before all lanes finish. If phase batching passes its correctness gates and improves rollout throughput by at least 25%, a second permission-gated slice may add an adaptive two-to-four-game pool.

Practical scaling conclusionThe fastest route to more evidence is likely several CPU seed processes plus parallel evaluation. The route to substantially larger single-run budgets is a vectorized collector. A GPU is the final stage of that collector work, not the first switch to flip.
