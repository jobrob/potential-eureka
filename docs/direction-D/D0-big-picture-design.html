01 · Executive decision

Direction D is the next project phase; pursue an exact batched substrate.

Direction A proved that the PPO method learns and generalizes under full rules. Direction D should now increase the amount of trustworthy experience one policy can consume in 48 hours. The recommended route is deliberately incremental: first make multiple games and partial trajectories a supported contract, then replace the object-heavy engine with phase-bucketed tensor state, then turn on CUDA. Every stage must earn continuation with exactness and end-to-end throughput.

Primary recommendation Implement an exact, phase-bucketed PyTorch tensor simulator behind a narrow environment interface. Reach it through a reusable cross-game collector rather than a one-shot engine rewrite. Keep the legacy engine as the semantic oracle and evaluation implementation throughout Direction D.
Current practical capacity~200Mnominal transitions per policy in 48 h
Direction D target≥3×end-to-end training throughput
Useful 48 h capacity≥600Mnominal transitions per policy
Stretch outcome4–8×with GPU inference and PPO updates

Not recommended as the first move: a monolithic GPU rewrite, a new distributed framework, or a learned model replacing exact rules. Each adds several uncertainties at once and makes correctness, speed, and learning effects difficult to attribute.

Parallel work: bot quality is tracked separately in the agent-strength investigation plan. Direction D does not assume that more transitions alone will make the current policy stronger.

03 · Contract

Speed is necessary, but exactness and learning safety are hard requirements.

  • Exact rules.
    The production PPO path must match the legacy engine for legal actions, stochastic outcomes, rewards, terminal order, and public/private observations.
  • 2–6 seats.
    No fast path may specialize to two players or assume zero-sum play.
  • Any generated track.
    Padded track tensors must preserve all generator output, not a curated fixed track set.
  • Imperfect information.
    Each policy observation exposes only the acting seat’s permitted information.
  • Deterministic identity.
    Game, track, policy-sampling, and stochastic-rule RNG streams remain independently reproducible.
  • Segmented full-state resume.
    Six planned 8-hour blocks must behave as one 48-hour run when stopped and resumed at safe PPO boundaries.
  • Bounded rollouts.
    The collector must stop without multiplying each PPO iteration by the number of live lanes.
  • Legacy oracle.
    The existing Python engine remains the source of truth for differential tests and final evaluation.
  • End-to-end measurement.
    Adoption is based on trained transitions per second, not an isolated kernel benchmark.

Explicit non-goals

Direction D does not change the reward, policy architecture, opponent mixture, checkpoint selection rule, or final skill gate. Those changes may be researched later, but mixing them into the substrate comparison would destroy attribution. It also does not authorize the final 48-hour campaign; that remains a separately reviewed experiment after the readiness gate.

05 · Trade-offs

Why the exact tensor route wins—and where the alternatives still fit.

Cross-game scheduling: necessary plumbing, not the destination

Run a bounded pool of independent exact games. Advance each lane until it needs a policy action, group ready observations, perform one batched policy call, and replay the actions through their lanes. Unlike T2, the batch comes from independent games, so it can reliably be 8–64 rather than 1–2.

The current collector computes GAE only after complete games. Direction D should replace that convenience with an explicit safe-boundary contract: freeze the rollout policy, stop admitting new work at the target transition count, advance each lane only until every pending seat action has received its reward and successor observation, then bootstrap nonterminal seat streams. This bounds overshoot without mixing pre-update and post-update policy data.

Multiprocess actors: strong CPU fallback, weaker primary architecture

Actors can run the unmodified engine in separate processes and send observation batches to a learner. This exploits CPU cores quickly, but policy versioning, queue backpressure, state serialization, and deterministic game identities become permanent complexity. It is attractive if the tensor kernel fails, or if CPU simulation remains the best measured route; it should not be introduced merely because distributed RL is familiar.

Compiled object engine: faster serial work, incomplete batching story

A Rust/C++ rewrite could make exact rules much faster and parallelize games with native threads. Cython or selective native kernels could target hotspots with less disruption. However, the current engine is roughly two thousand lines of branch-heavy rule logic backed by Python domain objects. Crossing the language boundary for every decision can preserve much of the plumbing cost. A native engine is credible, but it still needs the same lane scheduler and batched policy interface.

Exact tensor engine: one representation for rules, observations, and policy batches

Represent a batch of games with fixed-shape tensors: up to six seats, padded tracks, card-count arrays, per-seat phase state, RNG state, and validity masks. Group lanes by phase so gear selection, card play, movement, corner resolution, slipstream, and replenishment each run as a coherent masked kernel. Start on CPU for debugging, then move the unchanged tensor contract to CUDA.

PyTorch is recommended because the policy and PPO implementation already use it. This avoids a second tensor runtime and makes device-resident observations and policy calls possible. JAX remains a plausible alternative if a small spike demonstrates materially better compiled control flow without complicating the project’s normal Windows workflow.

Learned dynamics: high ceiling, weak first foundation

A stochastic latent model can predict rewards, terminal values, legal-action distributions, and a next latent state. It becomes especially valuable for cheap search. But Heat combines private hands, deck uncertainty, traffic interactions, and stochastic card draw: one-step accuracy can look good while multi-step policy-relevant errors compound. Learned transitions must therefore be treated as approximate computation and validated only against exact-engine play.

If this branch is opened, exact legality should remain authoritative, chance should be modeled probabilistically, and adoption should require exact-environment skill—not merely low prediction loss. It must never silently replace the final evaluation engine.

A training checkpoint must resume the session, not merely reload its policy

The current Direction-A checkpoint stores evaluable policy weights and architecture metadata only. Direction D adds a separate versioned training-state artifact. At a safe boundary after a PPO update it records:

State groupRequired contentsWhy it matters
Learning statePolicy and optimizer state, entropy-controller state, current hyperparameters, and policy version.Resetting Adam moments or the controller creates a warm start, not a continuation.
Campaign progressNominal/actual transitions, games, PPO iteration, elapsed training time, milestone/checkpoint ledger, and block number.Prevents repeated work, skipped gates, and ambiguous 48-hour accounting.
RandomnessNumPy generator state; PyTorch CPU and CUDA RNG state; track, seat-schedule, policy-sampling, and stochastic-rule stream state.The next game and update must have the same identity after restart.
Self-play contextRecent-opponent snapshot pool, anchor identity, opponent counters, and any curriculum/controller state.Resetting the opponent distribution can materially change training.
Compatibility receiptSchema version, resolved recipe, source revision/patch provenance, codec and engine versions, device/runtime metadata, and policy generation/run ID.Resume must fail fast rather than combine incompatible code or recipes.

Planned block endings stop after the next completed PPO update, drain the collector to its declared safe boundary, write to a temporary file, flush, atomically replace the destination, and store a SHA-256 receipt. Mid-iteration lane and rollout serialization is deliberately out of scope unless later operational evidence shows boundary-only checkpoints are insufficient.
