A9 - trainer integration

The A8 trainer can now choose the native collector. Each iteration freezes the live policy version, resolves current and snapshot rows through the A7 coordinator, advances complete native races, closes per-seat rewards, and bulk-finalizes the current-policy rows through the A8 arena before PPO mutates the policy.

  • Repeated identical manifests produced byte-identical PPO tensors.
  • Snapshot rows used their frozen policy but did not enter the live-policy PPO batch.
  • A complete two-iteration smoke updated the policy and preserved snapshot-controller counters.
  • The checkpoint records native manifest identity, next game and lease IDs, rows-per-game estimate, build/compiler receipt, worker count, and capacities.
Architecture complication. The result is a simpler bridge than the original design. NativePool workers currently validate and admit native manifests, while the Python coordinator thread calls the native rule methods for active games between batched policy calls. The rules themselves remain C++, but workers do not yet own and park live games at ready/action boundaries. Therefore A9 proves trainer integration, not completion of the originally drawn worker-owned hot loop.

A10 - bounded throughput rate passes adoption

The initial tuning scope used worker/slot counts 1, 2, 4, 8, and 12; Torch CPU thread counts 1, 2, 4, and 8; and rollout dispatch sizes 1,024, 4,096, and 8,192. The follow-up analysis correctly treated slots as bridge batch width and extended that grid to 24 slots and 16,384 rows. Both arms used the normal 256-by-256 flat masked policy, PPO batch 256, five epochs, the full 2-6 seat schedule, snapshot sampling, two Torch threads, and one discarded warm-up.

A10 - one-hour operational soak misses adoption

The authorized soak held the selected configuration for 3,600.20 seconds, completed 482 PPO updates, and trained 16,802,559 transitions. Sustained throughput was 4,667.12/s, below the 5,000/s adoption gate. Six equal iteration blocks had a 4,815.52/s median; the slowest reached only 3,988.74/s, or 82.83% of that median, so the 90% stability gate also failed.

The atomic checkpoint passed its SHA-256 and complete-state validation, no 60-second stall was recorded, and the longest PPO iteration was 12.36 seconds. Final RSS was 431.5 MiB and the 817.4 MiB peak was associated with transient checkpoint serialization. A post-run reporter bug lost the complete in-memory clock and memory series, so those two subgates are deliberately not called passes. The recovered receipt and limitation are recorded in the soak result.

A10 - bounded diagnosis selects a replacement

The mature hour split nearly evenly between current and snapshot opponents. Current-policy iterations averaged 5,196/s, while snapshot iterations averaged 4,245/s because mixed-policy dispatch reduced the mean action batch from 26.87 to 16.07 and doubled inference cost per row. A continuous telemetry run reproduced the broader slowdown without CPU clock collapse or Windows power throttling. Increasing Torch intra-op threads from two to eight materially reduced PPO cost; widening from 32 to 48 slots added a smaller steady-state batching gain.

The combined 48-slot, capacity-288, Torch-8/2 candidate trained 3,179,192 rows in 603.67 seconds, or 5,266.42/s. Its minimum six-window rate was 92.18% of the median, memory grew by only 3.16% after warm-up, CPU performance remained stable, no stall occurred, and the safe-boundary checkpoint passed. A concurrent-forward prototype was exact but only 1.001x at median, so it was removed.

A10 - replacement one-hour soak passes

The replacement trained 21,182,927 transitions in 3,605.28 seconds, or 5,875.53/s. Its six-window median was 5,874.56/s and the slowest window reached 5,806.34/s, or 98.84% of the median. It completed 616 PPO updates with no stall; post-warm-up memory growth was 21.1 MiB; the late/early CPU performance ratio was 99.26%; and the final checkpoint passed an independent SHA, recipe, source, and complete-state reload.

What would be needed to continue

The operational configuration can now be frozen at 48 slots, ready capacity 288, and Torch 8/2. The new-generation learning-quality gate is still required because the 32K rollout changes PPO update cadence. The eight-hour rehearsal, original worker-owned protocol, and CUDA/Option C remain separate decisions.
