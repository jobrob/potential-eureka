What the diagnostics found

Snapshot iterations were the first repeatable logical penalty. They averaged 4,245/s versus 5,196/s for current-policy iterations. Their action batches averaged 16.07 rows rather than 26.87, and inference cost was about twice as high per row. This explains why a three-iteration startup benchmark looked better than the mature 50% snapshot workload.

It was not a thermal or Windows power-throttling failure. During the continuous diagnostic, active-core performance stayed around 103-108% of nominal and Windows reported no execution-speed throttling, even as throughput dropped. Rollout, policy inference, and PPO update costs all rose, which points to insufficient CPU parallelism plus mixed-policy batch fragmentation rather than one bad native rule kernel.
