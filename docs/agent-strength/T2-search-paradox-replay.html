T2 · Investigation question

Why can one fixed search policy win over repaired play and lose to weak play?

The post-T1a T0 map contains exact matched coordinates where StaticSearchV1 finished first against a repaired field and did not finish first against a weak field. T2 replays both sides of those coordinates and records the search calculation, not just the terminal place.

Primary hypothesis · 70% confidenceThe same one-round raw-progress objective creates recognisable errors on both sides of the paradox. Opponent style changes blocking and traffic enough to decide whether those errors are punished; extra depth or more determinizations is not assumed to be the first repair.

The missed full-candidate plan had median production-score advantage 2.5 spaces; 147 misses were at least two spaces and 33 at least five. Its median prior rank was 13 (range 7–26). Fifteen misses across eight tight-track pairs and all 2/4/6-seat sizes were catastrophic under common seeds: the selected top-six plan spun in both traces while the pruned full-best plan did not.

Hypothesis verdict: rejected in its narrow form. A repeated cross-style bias does exist, but it is primarily the move_eval prior/top-six admission seam, not the one-round raw-progress leaf. The rollout-policy mismatch cannot be first because the admission failure appears in every paired weak and repaired replay. More determinizations also cannot be first: only 23 of 577 selected top-six plans (4.0%) lost to another admitted candidate under the common-seed control.

Concrete search failure: a safe line is pruned before rollout selection

On tight track 915002, two seats, game 1511202010, focal seat 1, round 17, search led at lap 2 position 24 with zero heat. The admitted gear-3 speed-12 plan crossed the corner and spun back to position 22 in both common-seed traces, scoring −978. The prior-ranked-13 gear-1 speed-2 plan cleared the same corner without spinning and scored +202. The exact pattern recurred on both opponent styles; this is an admission defect, not an argument for more search depth.

Clear sampled example. On default track 715008, two seats, game 1510208000, round 7, the car was at lap 2 position 4 with zero heat. The repaired heuristic chose a Stress card; both common-seed traces resolved speed 2, crossed the corner starting at 4, and spun back to position 3. Search chose the fixed value-1 card, cleared without heat, and retained position 5. This is not proof that every Stress draw spins, but it is a repeated, controllable risk error worth a separate heuristic investigation.

Finish-sequencing caution. Several search-only finishes exploit exact movement, blocking, and the later react phase. For example, on default track 715003 at lap 1 position 62, the search line was bounced to position 66 and then crossed on a boost, while the faster heuristic line crossed earlier and did not finish that round. Because this depends on lap/block/boost semantics, treat it as a rules/traffic audit lead rather than an immediate heuristic fix.

Decision unlocked

  • First search target: candidate admission.
    Design a bounded safety-aware prior/top-six experiment. Preserve exact production rollout scoring and test whether safe candidates can enter the six-plan set without widening latency excessively.
  • Second search target: leaf resources.
    After admission, test a small heat/rank tie-break on the preserved positions; do not start with a broad weight sweep.
  • Separate correctness repair.
    The search turn signature reused one stale plan at tight two seats: selected score −978 versus current admitted best −389.5. A round/hand-aware cache identity should receive its own semantic test.
  • Heuristic follow-up.
    Replay the repeated zero/low-heat Stress risks and audit finish sequencing. Do not infer universal superiority from the search shadow alone.
