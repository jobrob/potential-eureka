T1 · Investigation question

After the spin repair, does the heuristic still make a small number of recognisably bad choices?

H1 found one obvious recovery lock and repaired it. T0 now supplies a broader fixed loss population against both weak and search styles. T1 asks whether traffic, gear/card conservatism, slipstream, or finish timing explains a material share—and whether a human reader can point to concrete decisions that look wrong rather than merely different.

BoundaryT1 observes the current heuristic_repaired_v1. It does not change weights, safety rules, risk posture, discard logic, or opponent modelling. Proposed fixes remain designs until a later controlled experiment.

The 408 decisions split across default/tight tracks (160/248), weak/search opponents (222/186), and all three field sizes (134/148/126). What did not survive interpretation

Broad labels such as large_speed_sacrifice and clear_straight_underpush occurred in all 60 races, but many individual instances were reasonable responses to risk, heat, or card availability. Traffic displacement was context rather than a decision error. Repeated spins in several tight-track logs often occurred after the hand contained no non-spinning play, so those turns do not identify a new controllable repair.

A second implementation seam was observed: the plan-cache signature omits hand contents. It reused a stale plan on 37 turns in 23 races, but card revalidation recovered the current global best in every sampled case (zero measured planner regret). Treat this as a correctness hardening item, not the T1 strength repair.

ImplementedT1a stops forward-solvency projection at the finish line and reruns T0/T1 on preserved post-fix artifacts. The defect falls from 60/60 to 0/60 fresh losses. Cache-key hardening remains separate.
