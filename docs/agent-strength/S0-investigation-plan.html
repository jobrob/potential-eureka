01 · Structure

Training quality and static-bot quality should not share an intervention plan.

A learned agent can be weak because of data scale, PPO instability, self-play cycling, representation, value learning, or checkpoint selection. A static bot can be weak because of a deterministic rule, evaluator, search horizon, hidden-information approximation, or latency constraint. Combining those questions encourages fixes that improve the ruler rather than the learner—or train the learner to exploit one ruler’s defects.

Decision Maintain two independent investigation queues. Results may inform one another, but neither stream adopts an implementation change merely because it improves against the other stream’s current bot.

02 · Stream L

Learned-agent strength: is the training process producing broader skill?

G0002 passed A8’s untouched final gate, but training is short, oscillatory, seed-sensitive, and not calibrated against humans. G0003 and G0004 contain promising skill signals without earning adoption.

Learned-agent investigation plan Current evidence, unknowns, and investigations for scale, replay, self-play dynamics, representation, learning signals, and policy combination.
03 · Stream T

Static-bot strength: can fixed heuristic and search agents become reliable opponents?

The repaired heuristic fixed a severe spin loop but is not universally stronger than the weak bot. StaticSearchV1 beats the repaired heuristic yet loses to weak fields. The static stream must understand that non-transitivity before changing weights, search depth, or labels.

Static-bot investigation plan Current bot inventory, deterministic failure evidence, and investigations for heuristics, search leaves, budgets, exploitability, track regimes, and latency.
04 · Shared evaluation contract

The streams share measurements, not implementation decisions.

  • Multi-ruler results.
    No single bot becomes the definition of strength; aggregates retain every per-ruler result.
  • Matched conditions.
    Track, race, starting-seat, and chance seeds match when contenders are compared.
  • 2–6-seat relevance.
    Headline claims retain at least the established 2/4/6-seat slices.
  • Track-family slices.
    Generated-track geometry and congestion remain visible rather than disappearing into one average.
  • Development versus final.
    The A8 final band remains historical. Investigations use new declared development data; frozen candidates later receive fresh untouched data.
  • Human calibration later.
    A competent-human reference would inform both streams, but informal games cannot become a formal gate.
  • Latency alongside strength.
    Static search reports decision cost; learned policies report inference cost under the same hardware receipt.
  • Promotion requires breadth.
    A change that farms one opponent style while regressing another is recorded as a matchup change, not universal improvement.
05 · Recommended order

Calibrate the rulers, then diagnose the learner against the resulting population.

  1. T0
    map static-bot matchups
  2. T1/T2
    diagnose heuristic and search failures
  3. L0/L1
    map and replay learned-policy losses
  4. Design
    one intervention in one stream

This order does not require finishing every static-bot idea before studying G0002. It only establishes which fixed opponents are useful measurement instruments before their scores drive a learned-agent redesign. Direction D may proceed independently throughout.
