Are the apparent static-agent reversals stable regimes or small-sample accidents?

B1 showed StaticSearchV1 beating repaired fields while losing placement value to weak fields. H1 showed the repaired heuristic near or below the weak style depending on seat count. Decision unlocked

  • Static population.
    Retain all three identities. Weak play is not merely a floor, repaired play contributes a distinct economy style, and search contributes a geometry-sensitive simulation style.
  • T1 heuristic replay.
    Contrast repaired-agent losses to weak fields on tight six-seat tracks (mean −0.094) with its positive default four-seat cell (+0.083). This is more informative than replaying one pooled heuristic sample.
  • T2 search replay.
    Use the six-seat Search → Weak default/tight contrast (−0.033 versus +0.328), alongside Search → Repaired (+0.350/+0.467), to isolate geometry and opponent-policy effects.
  • Starting position remains a stratifier.
    Some 12-game seat slices span more than 0.5 reward, including tight six-seat Search → Weak (+0.067 to +0.667). Those slices locate replays but are too small for seat-specific tuning claims.
06 · Reliability and limits

The map selects diagnostics; it does not promote a champion.

  • One race per track × focal seat.
    Cells contain 24, 48, or 72 races, but each exact coordinate has one game seed. Near-zero effects require replay or a powered confirmation before becoming strength claims.
  • First-place uncertainty is retained.
    Many Wilson intervals cross the chance line. Placement reward is the primary map signal; T0 does not convert its sign into a formal pass/fail gate.
  • Homogeneous fields are intentional.
    They expose style interactions cleanly but do not estimate performance in a mixed tournament population.
  • Consumed development bands.
    715000+ and 915000+ are now static-benchmark development data, not untouched final-test tracks.
  • No learned-policy conclusion.
    T0 contains no learned contender and does not modify the policy-generation registry.
